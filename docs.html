InkLook docs

Everything you need to get InkLook running and to bend it to your game. The same docs ship inside the package as README.md, so you can read them offline too.

InkLook started as the look of my own game, Before the Ink Runs. I pulled it out so it works in any URP project.

Before you start

  • Unity 6 (6000.0 or newer). I build and test on 6000.3.
  • URP 17. Built-in and HDRP are not supported.
  • The core needs nothing else. The demo scenes use TextMeshPro, which is part of Unity UI.

What's in the box:

FolderWhat it holds
Shaders/Object shading, the full-screen ink pass, brush strokes, particles, the scene transition and the canvas shaders
Runtime/All the components and the scripting API (assembly InkLook.Runtime)
Editor/The Tools > Ink Look menu, plus record and replay buttons on the canvas inspector
Recordings/Enso and Slash, ready to play as effects
Demo/Two demo scenes and their scripts. Delete it if you don't need it.
Textures/The paper grain, brush and splat alphas, and two seals
Resources/InkLook/Transition settings, and the canvas resources that make runtime canvases work in builds

Setup

Four steps, about two minutes.

  1. Import the package, or copy the InkLook folder into Assets/. It also works as an embedded package in Packages/; the package.json is there.
  2. Run Tools > Ink Look > Add Ink Pass to Renderer. It uses the renderer you have selected in the Project window, or the default renderer of your URP asset. You get your own copy of the ink material at Assets/InkLookSettings/M_InkPost.mat.
  3. In each scene, run Tools > Ink Look > Add Ink World to Scene. It adds the object that feeds the shaders (paper colour, grain, distance fade) and the brush-stroke spawner, and makes the camera background paper-coloured.
  4. Give your objects ink materials with Assets > Create > Ink Look > Ink Lit Material. Each one has three tones: light, mid and shade.

Want to see it first? Open the demo scenes in Demo/Scenes. InkLookDemo has a button for every effect (it needs the ink pass on the default renderer). InkCanvasDemo is the painting demo. If Unity asks, import TMP Essential Resources.

The look

The look is made of a few parts that you can use together or on their own.

Ink Lit (object shading)

Objects get three flat tones instead of smooth lighting, the way an ink painter lays down a few washes. A little grain noise wobbles the border between tones so it looks like a brush edge, and objects fade into the paper with distance and towards their feet.

Ink Post (the full-screen pass)

One full-screen pass turns the frame into a painting: outlines from depth and normals, brush pressure along the lines, a slight hand wobble, dry-brush breakup, a soft wash beside each line, paper grain and a light vignette. Lines fade out with distance, so far scenery is drawn in wash only.

InkWorld

The InkWorld component holds the paper colour and distance fade for the scene, and drives the full-screen moments below.

Brush strokes and the transition

BrushFx paints arcs, rings, streaks, bursts and splats in the world. SceneTransition paints the screen over with ink, swaps the scene, then wipes it away. Brush strokes use unscaled time, so they keep playing during hit-stop or slow motion.

Effects from code

using InkLook;

//painted scene change (works with nothing in the scene)
SceneTransition.Go("Level01");
SceneTransition.Run(() => player.Respawn());               //cover, do something, reveal
SceneTransition.CoverStarted += () => audio.Play("brush"); //hook your own sounds

//full-screen moments, 0..1
InkWorld.Flash = 1f;        //flash of bare paper, fade it out yourself
InkWorld.Invert = 1f;       //ink negative
InkWorld.ScreenBleed = 0.5f;//wet stains creeping in from the edges
InkWorld.Dissolve = 1f;     //the picture washes into the paper

//brush strokes in the world (side-on, drawn at BrushFx.zPlane)
BrushFx.Arc(centre, radius, fromDeg, toDeg, width, BrushFx.Ink, life);
BrushFx.Ring(centre, radius, width, BrushFx.Ink, life, grow);
BrushFx.Streak(a, b, width, BrushFx.Wash, life);
BrushFx.Burst(centre, count, inner, outer, width, BrushFx.Ink, life);
BrushFx.Splat(centre, size, BrushFx.Ink, life);

The ink canvas

InkCanvas is a sheet of paper you paint on. The ink is simulated on the GPU: it bleeds into the paper while wet, dries with a slightly darker rim, and breaks into dry-brush streaks when the brush runs out over a long stroke. It works on its own, so you don't need the ink pass or InkWorld for it.

Three ways to paint

  • On screen (UI): put a RawImage in a Canvas and add InkCanvasView. It shows the sheet, keeps its aspect and paints with mouse, touch or pen. Pen pressure changes the width. You need an EventSystem in the scene.
  • On a 3D object: add InkCanvasSurface to an object with a MeshCollider, like a quad for a scroll or a paper screen. The camera needs a PhysicsRaycaster.
  • From code: feed it strokes yourself, in sheet coordinates (0..1, origin bottom-left).
var sheet = GetComponent<InkCanvas>();
sheet.brush = InkBrush.Dry();            //presets: Bold(), Wet(), Dry(), Fine(), Splash()
sheet.BeginStroke(uv, pressure);         //brush down
sheet.MoveStroke(uv, pressure);          //every time the pointer moves
sheet.EndStroke();                       //brush up, a fast stroke ends in a flick
sheet.Stroke(listOfPoints);              //a whole stroke at once
sheet.Splash(uv, direction, 40f, 1f);    //blot, spikes and drops
sheet.StampSeal(sealTexture, uv, 60f);   //press a seal, vermilion by default

sheet.Undo(); sheet.Clear(); sheet.DryNow();
string file = sheet.SavePNG();           //persistentDataPath, or pass a path
Texture2D copy = sheet.Capture();        //a readable copy, destroy it when done
rawImage.texture = sheet.Texture;        //the live sheet (a RenderTexture)

Brushes

The five presets are just different values of the same settings, so start from one and tweak.

SettingWhat it does
sizeBrush width in sheet pixels
inkHow dark the stroke is
waterHow much it bleeds into the paper
drynessHow streaky the bristles are
inkReachHow far it goes before running dry
speedThinningFast strokes get thinner, like a lifted brush
pressureHow much pen pressure widens the line
startTaper / flickThe thin start, and the point a quick lift leaves
smoothingA lazy brush that turns shaky input into clean curves
spatterDrops and spikes thrown off fast strokes
hardnessFrom a soft round tip to a crisp edge
tint0 = first ink, 1 = second ink, anything between mixes

The sheet itself has its own settings: resolution, paper and ink colours, grain, density, and how the wet ink behaves (flow, evaporation, settle, edge settle for the dark rim, pin, fibre, mobility, steps per second and undo levels).

Two inks and seals

Every canvas has two inks: ink (sumi) and ink2 (vermilion by default). A brush picks one with tint. The two mix where they meet, and the second ink bleeds and dries just like the first.

StampSeal(texture, uv, size, angle, tint) presses a seal using the texture's alpha, with uneven paste and missed specks. Two seals come in Textures/, and any texture with alpha works as your own seal. InkCanvasView stamps on click when its tool is set to Seal.

Recording and replay

A recording stores what the hand did, not the picture. When you replay it, the simulation paints it again, wet and bleeding, with the same spatter and specks.

sheet.StartRecording();
//...paint...
InkRecording r = sheet.StopRecording();                 //pauses over 0.6 s get shortened
sheet.Play(r);                                          //draws it back, animated
sheet.Play(r, new Rect(0.6f, 0.1f, 0.3f, 0.3f), 2f);    //into a corner, twice as fast
string json = r.ToJson();                               //save it anywhere
var again = InkRecording.FromJson(json, seals);         //and load it back
  • Several recordings can play at once, and the player can keep painting while they do.
  • In Play mode the canvas inspector has Start / Stop recording, Replay and Save as asset (saved to Assets/InkLookSettings/Recordings).
  • You can also write recordings in code with AddStroke, AddSplash, AddSeal and AddClear. That's how I made the included Enso and Slash.
  • InkPlayer plays a recording on the canvas next to it, on start and looping if you want. Good for calligraphy that writes itself on a title screen.

Ink as game effects

On the screen

InkScreenFx lays a see-through canvas over the whole screen. Ink lands, bleeds, dries and fades by itself. It makes itself the first time you use it; add one to a scene only if you want to change its settings.

InkScreenFx.Splash(screenPoint, screenDirection, 60f, 1f);           //a big hit
InkScreenFx.SplashAtWorld(hit.point, attackDirection, tint: 1f);     //vermilion, where the hit is on screen
InkScreenFx.Play(slashRecording, new Rect(0.05f, 0.1f, 0.9f, 0.8f)); //a finisher stroke across the screen

In the world

InkSplashSurface on an object with a MeshCollider and an InkCanvas splashes ink where things hit it. It reacts to collisions (faster hits splash bigger), or you call Hit(point, direction, strength) for bullets, raycasts and attacks.

For ink without a paper sheet, turn on transparentPaper on the canvas and give the object the InkLook/Canvas/Decal material, laid just over a floor or wall.

Any canvas can be an effect. transparentPaper draws only the ink, fadePerSecond makes new ink fade away, and FadeAway(1f) fades out everything already there, say to clean a floor after a fight.

Tuning

To changeGo to
Paper, outlines, wash, wobble, vignetteAssets/InkLookSettings/M_InkPost.mat
Paper colour, distance fadeThe InkWorld component
Transition timings and soundsTools > Ink Look > Edit Transition Settings

I tuned the outline thresholds (Depth Threshold, Line Fade) for a 30° camera 15 to 20 m from the action. If your camera is much closer or further, retune those two first.

Performance

  • The look costs one full-screen pass per frame.
  • A canvas costs nothing while it's dry. While wet, it runs up to 12 small simulation steps per frame.
  • A 1600 × 1000 canvas uses about 32 MB, plus about 13 MB per undo level in use (8 by default). Effect canvases use 0 undo levels.
  • The screen effect canvas runs at 60% of screen resolution. Lower it, or the undo levels, on mobile.

Troubleshooting

Nothing looks inked

The ink pass isn't on the renderer your camera uses. Select that renderer in the Project window and run Add Ink Pass to Renderer again, then check the camera's Renderer dropdown.

Outlines are too thick, too thin or flicker

Your camera distance is different from what I tuned for. Adjust Depth Threshold and Line Fade on M_InkPost.

I can't paint on the canvas

On UI: add an EventSystem. On a 3D object: make sure it has a MeshCollider and the camera has a PhysicsRaycaster.

Canvases work in the editor but not in a build

Keep Resources/InkLook/InkCanvasResources.asset in the project. Runtime canvases find their shaders through it.

The demo title is missing

Import TMP Essential Resources when Unity asks.
